# Interplanetary Survival Guide

These rules travel with every EVA sheet. The route, your **return-by time** and your **point of no return** are printed above them from today's plan. The copilot applies the same rules live; this page is what you do if you have to decide alone.

## The limits

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Oxygen when you reach the habitat | **at least 30%** left |
| Back at the habitat | **30 min before sunset** at the latest; dust brings sunset earlier |
| Steepest ground you walk on | **20°** |
| Radiation dose per EVA | **1 mSv** at most (a normal 5-hour EVA receives about 0.14 mSv) |
| Help from Earth | about **27 min** on 14 Nov 2026 (11 min each way + 5 min to react); between ~11 and ~50 min, depending on where the planets are |

## Before you leave

- Read the three numbers on this sheet: return-by time, point of no return, oxygen left on arrival.
- If a regional dust storm is forecast from orbit, **do not start the EVA**.
- Large dust devils peak around 12:00-13:00 local time: plan the walk around that window when you can.

## Your three numbers

- **Return-by time:** start walking home by then, whatever is left to do. No sample is worth it.
- **Point of no return:** past it, your oxygen only covers the way home. Do not add stops after it.
- **Oxygen on arrival:** if the copilot's estimate falls below 30%, turn back now.

---

## Dust devil

- Ignore small ones (under ~30 m): they pass in seconds.
- Keep clear of large ones (over 100 m, internal winds up to 25 m/s). Follow the copilot's detour: it keeps you outside the forecast cone.
- If no detour is safe, stop and let it pass, then continue.

## Storm front (local dust storm)

- You cannot walk around it: a front is tens of kilometres wide.
- If you can reach the habitat before it arrives, **go now**. If not, stop where you are and wait until it passes (a 5 km thick front at 15 m/s passes in about 6 min), then head home.
- In dust, follow the planned route, not what you see. Sunset comes about 30 min earlier for each unit of dust opacity above 0.5, so your return-by time moves earlier too.

## Regional dust storm

- **Return immediately**, by the planned route home. It does not pass in hours; it lasts days.
- Do not stop for samples on the way back.

## Solar storm (solar particle event)

- The dose rises over about 2 hours, up to about 2.5 times normal (the largest event measured on the surface, September 2017).
- **Go to shelter** (the habitat, or any shelter on your route) or **return**. Stay inside until the habitat gives the all-clear: the dose falls slowly, over about a day and a half.

## Suit leak

- **Return now**, by the shortest way home. Do not finish the stop.
- A leak burns extra oxygen (about 0.15% per minute in our model). The copilot recomputes your reserve with the leak; if you would arrive with less than 30%, it declares an emergency.

## Fatigue

- Tell the copilot. It replans for 80% of your speed and 115% of your oxygen use.
- Drop the farthest stop first. Never walk past the return-by time to finish a sample.

## Marsquake

- Stop. Keep away from steep slopes and the foot of the delta front.
- Do not cross the zone the copilot marks around the epicentre (about 300 m): detour or return.

## Loss of contact with the habitat

- Keep following the last plan. The return-by time and the point of no return do not need a link.
- The copilot decides on board, with no help from Earth; if it is down too, turn back at your return-by time or at the first sign of a hazard, whichever comes first.

---

**When in doubt, go home.** The copilot does the same: in our benchmark it brought fewer samples back (41% vs 100%) and the astronaut home safely more often (81% vs 51%).
